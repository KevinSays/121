Phishing Triage

Four messages, flagged by an automated filter and dropped on your desk at Bastion Security's client, Halcyon Logistics. All four are fictional, written for this exercise — but the patterns are real. Your job: decide which ones are actually phishing, and defend the call.

Companion to Session 1 Time ~30 min Difficulty Beginner
↩ Contents Objectives The Messages Worksheet Reflection

Objectives

What you'll do

  • Identify sender-domain inconsistencies and other technical tells
  • Name the psychological lever each message relies on, using Session 1's list
  • Practice not flagging a legitimate message just because it landed in the batch
Not everything here is phishing. Real inboxes are mostly legitimate mail — over-flagging everything is its own failure mode. Read each message the way you'd actually read your own inbox, not the way you'd read a quiz that told you "these are all phishing."

Read each one before looking at the worksheet below. Domains and buttons are illustrative only — nothing here is a real, clickable link.

Worksheet

Triage each message

Use Session 1's Social Engineering table and psychology-levers list. "Sender domain" means: does it actually match who it claims to be from?

MessageSender domain OK?Psychology lever(s)Verdict
1 — "IT Helpdesk"
2 — "Marcus Reyes"
3 — "Delivery Notifications"
4 — "HR Team"
Look closely at Message 2's sender domain, letter by letter. A convincing Business Email Compromise doesn't need a sloppy fake — often just one transposed letter in a domain most people won't check closely under time pressure.

Reflection

Write it up

1.Which message was hardest to judge, and why? What single piece of information — if you'd had it — would have made the call obvious?

2.Message 2 never asks for a password or a click — no link, no attachment, no credential harvesting. Why is it still dangerous, and why might it be more dangerous than Message 1?

3.If Message 4 had been the one flagged as suspicious by an automated filter (false positive), what's the cost of a real employee getting used to ignoring flagged messages?
