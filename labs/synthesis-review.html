Session 1 Synthesis Review

Eight short scenarios. Each one gets run through every framework from Session 1 at once — CIA triad, threat/vulnerability/exploit/risk, threat actor, and malware or social-engineering technique where they apply. Some scenarios won't have an answer for every field, on purpose — figuring out when information genuinely isn't there is part of the skill.

Companion to Session 1 Time ~35–45 min Difficulty Review / capstone

Objectives

What you'll do

  • Apply the CIA triad, threat/vulnerability/exploit/risk chain, threat actor categories, and malware/social-engineering types to short scenarios
  • Practice classifying an incident using more than one framework at once, the way a real case rarely fits neatly into a single table
  • Recognize when a scenario doesn't give you enough information to answer a field with confidence
How to use this: for each scenario, fill in every field you can support with something in the text. If a field genuinely isn't answerable — no motive given, no mechanism described — write "not enough information" rather than guessing. That's a legitimate, correct answer here.

Case Load

Eight scenarios

Scenario 1

A hospital's imaging system suddenly displays a ransom note. All patient scan files are encrypted and inaccessible until payment is made.

Scenario 2

An employee, two days from being terminated, uses their still-active account to permanently delete the project files their team has spent the year building.

Scenario 3

A company runs a web server with a publicly known, unpatched vulnerability. Within hours of the vulnerability's public disclosure, someone downloads a ready-made exploit tool and uses it to break in.

Scenario 4

A political advocacy group defaces a city government website with a protest message. The site is restored within hours. No data was accessed or stolen.

Scenario 5

An employee gets a phone call from someone claiming to be from IT, saying there's an urgent security issue that requires the employee's password to fix immediately.

Scenario 6

A free "photo editor" app, downloaded from an untrusted site, turns out to secretly record everything the user types, including their banking password.

Scenario 7

A defense contractor discovers that a foreign intelligence service has had quiet, undetected access to their network for over a year, slowly gathering internal documents.

Scenario 8

A company's e-commerce site goes offline for six hours after being flooded with traffic from thousands of compromised devices simultaneously.

Reflection

Write it up

1.Which scenario(s) did you mark "not enough information" for the threat actor field? What specific piece of missing information would have let you answer confidently?

2.Scenario 6 has both a delivery mechanism and a separate payload — two different malware types working together. Explain the difference in your own words, using this scenario as the example.

3.Scenarios 2 and 7 both involve someone with legitimate or long-standing access. What's actually different about how you'd respond to each one?
